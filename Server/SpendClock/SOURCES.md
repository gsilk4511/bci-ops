# Construction layer sources
- Housing: Census BPS annual 2022–25, monthly Oct 2025 and Oct 2026 YTD; Northeast permit-issuing place files and U.S. county files. Count each county once for Greater Burlington and statewide. Place IDs are not census geographic IDs. Publish raw and directly reported units separately. Monthly/annual feed validation rejects missing areas.
- Local economics: 2020–24 ACS summary tables B01003, B25001, B25003, B19013; 2023 Vermont LEHD LODES8 WAC S000 JT02 private jobs, assigned to county subdivisions with VT block crosswalk. Commercial allocation proxy includes wholesale, retail, information, finance, real estate, professional, management, admin, private education and health, arts, food/accommodation, and other services. Industrial proxy uses manufacturing only. These data are not construction spend. Metro household income is not calculated by averaging medians.
- State anchors: annual Census construction by state official XLSX (private nonresidential and separately state/local). Formula shown on the site. Private anchor includes nonbuilding spending and facility types not scoped by BCI, and the small-state estimate has considerable sampling error. BCI low-voltage percentages and commercial/industrial new/renovation splits are illustrative and editable, not published empirical averages.
- Owner-occupied renovation baseline: JCHS 2025 Using 2023 AHS national $4,700 per owner. Rental owners and seasonal units are not represented. Housing permit values and actual state construction outlays have different definitions; do not add the public anchor to the local model.
- RSS carries reported permit releases, not forecasts or live consumer payments. Market model settings are browser-local. JSON carries the published observations and seed assumptions for reuse.

# Five-state and national coverage
- VT, NH, NY, CT and MA clocks use the Census county and place annual and same-period YTD Building Permits Survey files. The market lists use population-ranked Census county subdivisions/places, with county-based regional groupings and intentional local inclusions. Census ACS 2020–24 and LEHD/LODES 2023 remain named baseline snapshots. Connecticut's 2022 statewide county rollup uses historical counties; its newer planning-region geography begins in 2023. Counties and towns can overlap region rows and must not be added a second time.
- Statewide private nonresidential and public spending anchors check their Census annual workbooks; state construction payroll jobs check BLS/FRED. Local trade estimates for new and renovation work allocate by service and manufacturing employment proxies. Five additional trade ranges are configurable scenario percentages, not published local market shares. The original low-voltage assumptions remain separately configurable by construction segment.
- Commercial/industrial count cards show only independently checked municipal permit data with city/district coverage and dates. They are not a complete federal permit series and not counts of completed buildings. Do not extrapolate from a city to a region or state, or convert spend into counts. Missing data is unknown, not zero.
- The nationwide map is an interactive 2025 Census snapshot for all 50 states and D.C., using permit counts and values, private nonresidential and state/local construction spending, and Census state population estimates. It is not a live feed of projects.
- RSS defaults to Vermont for compatibility; choose other market permit releases with ?state=nh, ny, ct, ma, or all. The JSON endpoint contains the five-state model inputs, local permit coverage, and source periods.

# Nationwide clock coverage
- All 50 states and Washington, D.C. have statewide clocks. County-market and city/town drilldowns are selected population-ranked locations, not full coverage of every municipality. Areas with suppressed or unavailable inputs show gaps. National statewide construction jobs use BLS State Employment and Unemployment Table 3 (October 2026 archive, checked against the current release). Alaska and Michigan local workplace baselines may differ in year or survey; follow labels in market details. The national heat map remains a labeled 2025 source snapshot.
- Vermont has all 14 county rows. Vermont Tourism’s Northwest, Northeast Kingdom, Central, and Southern groups partition all 14 counties; Greater Burlington and convenience markets are separate, overlapping lenses and are not additive. Towns may belong to more than one menu view.
- Colchester publishes a category called New Commercial, which can include additions or renovations. Calendar 2025 and Jan–Oct 2026 both yielded two permits from the monthly reports; this context lives in source details only, not the unique-building count.
